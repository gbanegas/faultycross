\revon
We classified faults in seed-tree-based signatures with a six-component
GZKST framework and identified a cursor-dependent publication vulnerability
in MEDS that does not arise in the previously studied flag-array traversal.
Three clock-glitch experiments demonstrate root, subtree and hidden-leaf
publication faults in an instrumented seed-path routine. Given an emitted
signature that contains a hidden seed and its response, the resulting matrix
relations yield signing-equivalent secret information. The full-component
coverage probabilities and near-one effective-query expectations apply to
\emph{root disclosure} under an explicit independent-uniform challenge
assumption, not to arbitrary partial disclosures.

The measurements are function-level: complete-device signing,
end-to-end extraction, the leak distribution of the cursor fault and a
correlated double-glitch verification bypass remain untested. The results
therefore motivate both implementation-specific hardening and the evaluation
of independent release-time checks under realistic faults, rather than an
unqualified claim of a practical full-signing compromise.
