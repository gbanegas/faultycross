\begin{abstract}
\revon
Fiat--Shamir post-quantum signatures use GGM seed trees to compress
per-round randomness. A fault during seed publication can expose a seed
for a hidden round alongside its response, allowing long-term secret
extraction. We present the Generic ZK Seed Tree (GZKST), a framework for
classifying such fault surfaces, and use it to analyse an overlooked
cursor-dependent path traversal in the MEDS reference implementation.
For a complete root-seed disclosure, under independent uniform nonzero
challenge indices, one faulted signature reveals signing-equivalent
secret information with probability at least $0.987$ across the listed
MEDS parameter sets. We demonstrate three clock-glitch effects on a
Cortex-M4 using an instrumented, isolated seed-path routine: root,
subtree, and hidden-leaf disclosure. These are function-level experiments,
not an end-to-end signing or key-recovery demonstration. We examine
countermeasures and identify a potential correlated-fault risk when
path construction and reconstruction share code.
\keywords{GGM trees \and Post-quantum signatures \and Fault injection \and Seed compression}
\revoff
\end{abstract}
