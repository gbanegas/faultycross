\begin{abstract}
Post-quantum signatures built from Fiat--Shamir transforms of zero-knowledge
identification protocols---including LESS, CROSS, MEDS, and the
MPC-in-the-Head family---compress their ephemeral randomness with a GGM seed
tree, publishing only the seeds of the revealed rounds while hiding the
challenge-dependent ones. Deciding, per node, which seeds may be released is
the single point at which zero knowledge is enforced in the implementation,
and it is a fault-attack surface.
We introduce the Generic ZK Seed Tree (GZKST), an abstraction of seed-tree
generation, challenge partitioning, and seed publication, together with the
signature scheme derived from it, and formalise its correctness and
seed-hiding invariants. Classifying the published fault attacks on this family
by which component of the abstraction they corrupt shows that five independent
attacks against four schemes occupy only three of six components; the empty
cells are predictions, and one of them led us to a fault surface in the MEDS
reference implementation with no analogue in prior work.
We derive generic key-recovery algorithms from this view and bound the number
of effective faulted signatures required for full recovery---at most $s-1
\leq 5$ for every MEDS parameter set. We then demonstrate the attacks in
practice through clock-glitch fault injection against the MEDS reference
implementation on an ARM Cortex-M4 (ChipWhisperer-Lite), identifying three
exploitable surfaces in tree traversal and path construction that yield
complete tree disclosure, partial subtree recovery, or leakage of individual
hidden leaves. Finally, we evaluate countermeasures, and show that the obvious
defence---verifying a signature before releasing it---is weaker than it looks
here, because path construction and path reconstruction share a single
function in the reference code.
\keywords{GGM tree \and Zero-knowledge signatures \and Fault attacks \and
Post-quantum cryptography \and Seed compression}
\end{abstract}
