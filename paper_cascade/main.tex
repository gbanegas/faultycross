\begin{abstract}
Post-quantum signatures built by applying Fiat--Shamir to a parallel-repeated
zero-knowledge identification protocol---LESS, CROSS, MEDS and the
MPC-in-the-Head family among them---compress their ephemeral randomness with a
GGM seed tree, publishing the seeds of the revealed rounds and hiding the
challenge-dependent ones. Deciding, per node, which seeds may be released is
the single point at which zero knowledge is enforced in the implementation,
and it is a fault-attack surface.
We introduce the Generic ZK Seed Tree (GZKST), an abstraction of seed-tree
generation, challenge partitioning and seed publication together with the
signature scheme derived from it, and formalise its correctness and
seed-hiding invariants. Classifying the published fault attacks on this family
by which component they corrupt shows five independent attacks against four
schemes occupying only three of six components; the empty cells are
predictions, and one of them led us to a fault surface in the MEDS reference
implementation with no analogue in prior work. We derive generic key-recovery
algorithms and show that at most $s-1 \leq 5$ effective faulted signatures
suffice for every MEDS parameter set. We then demonstrate three clock-glitch
attacks against that implementation on an ARM Cortex-M4, yielding complete
tree disclosure, subtree recovery, or leakage of individual hidden leaves, and
evaluate countermeasures---including the observation that verifying before
releasing is weaker than it looks here, since path construction and
reconstruction share one function.
\keywords{GGM tree \and Zero-knowledge signatures \and Fault attacks \and
Post-quantum cryptography \and Seed compression}
\end{abstract}
