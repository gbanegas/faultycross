\begin{abstract}
Post-quantum signature schemes derived from Fiat-Shamir transformations of 
zero-knowledge identification protocols frequently rely on GGM-tree-based 
seed compression to reduce signature size. This mechanism is used in several 
recent constructions, including LESS, CROSS, and MEDS, where only 
challenge-dependent subsets of ephemeral seeds are hidden while the 
remaining seeds are disclosed for verification. Although essential for 
efficiency, this design introduces a fault-attack surface: faults affecting 
the seed-publication logic may reveal hidden seeds together with their 
associated zero-knowledge responses, enabling recovery of secret information. 
In this work, we introduce the Generic ZK Seed Tree (GZKST) abstraction, 
a unified framework capturing the common structure of GGM-tree generation, 
challenge partitioning, and seed publication across these schemes. Within this 
model, we formalize correctness and seed-hiding invariants and show that previously 
proposed attacks against LESS-v1 and LESS-v2 correspond to violations of the 
same underlying invariant despite targeting different implementation layers 
and tree constructions. Using this abstraction, we derive generic key-recovery 
algorithms and analyze the number of effective faulted signatures required for 
complete secret recovery. For all MEDS parameter sets, we show that only a 
small number of successful faulted queries is sufficient in practice. We 
additionally demonstrate the practicality of these attacks through clock-glitch 
fault injections against the MEDS reference implementation on an ARM Cortex-M4 
microcontroller using a ChipWhisperer-Lite platform. Our experiments identify 
multiple exploitable fault surfaces in the tree traversal and path-construction 
logic, enabling complete tree disclosure, partial subtree recovery, or leakage 
of hidden leaves.
\keywords{GGM tree \and Zero-knowledge signatures \and Fault attacks \and 
Post-quantum cryptography \and Seed compression}
\end{abstract}
